① 肯定の命令文Affirmative commands Oraciones imperativas afirmativas

スペイン語で固有の命令形を持つのは tú と vosotros だけです。それ以外の人称はすべて接続法現在（9課）をそのまま使います。

Only tú and vosotros have true imperative forms in Spanish. Every other person simply borrows the present subjunctive (Lesson 9). English has one command form for everyone (“Speak!”), so the first decision in Spanish is always: who am I talking to?

※ 3人称＝usted / ustedes への丁寧な命令。1人称複数＝nosotros「〜しましょう」。

※ 3rd person = polite commands to usted / ustedes. 1st person plural = nosotros “let’s …”.

1. tú に対する命令形1. Commands to tú

直説法現在3人称単数形と同形です。ただし以下の8語は不規則形を持ちます。

They look exactly like the present indicative 3rd person singular (habla “he speaks” → ¡Habla! “speak!”). Eight verbs have irregular forms:

venir（来る） (come)vendecir（言う） (say/tell)di
ir（行く） (go)veser（〜である） (be)sé

※ ve（ir の命令）と ve（ver の直説法3単）、sé（ser の命令）と sé（saber の直説法1単）は同形。文脈で判断する。

※ ve (command of ir) = ve (“he sees”, from ver); sé (command of ser, “be!”) = sé (“I know”, from saber). Context tells them apart. Memory hook: the eight short forms are mostly the infinitive with its ending chopped off (pon-er, sal-ir, ten-er, ven-ir).

3. 目的格代名詞・再帰代名詞の位置3. Where object and reflexive pronouns go

肯定命令では動詞の後ろに続けてつける（1語になる）。アクセント位置が変わらないよう、アクセント記号が必要になる場合があります。

In affirmative commands the pronouns are attached to the end of the verb, forming one word — just like English puts them after the verb (“Help us”, “Tell me it”), except Spanish writes them together. An accent mark is often needed so that the stress stays where it was.

Ayúdanos a llevar estas cajas.この箱を運ぶのを手伝って。Help us carry these boxes.ayuda ＋ nos
Si necesitas algo, dímelo.何か必要なら、私にそれを言って。If you need anything, tell me (it).di ＋ me ＋ lo
アクセント記号のルールThe accent-mark rule
  • ayuda（a-YU-da）に nos が付くと音節が1つ増える → 元の位置を保つため ayúdanos
  • di は1音節だが、代名詞が2つ付くと dímelo（DÍ-me-lo）となり記号が必要
  • 語順は3課・5課と同じ「再帰 → 間接 → 直接」（dí-me-lo）
  • 否定命令では逆に動詞の前に置く（→②）
  • ayuda (a-YU-da) + nos adds a syllable → write ayúdanos to keep the stress on YU
  • di has one syllable, but with two pronouns it becomes dímelo (DÍ-me-lo) and needs the mark
  • Pronoun order is the same as in Lessons 3 and 5: reflexive → indirect → direct (dí-me-lo = “tell-me-it”, the same order as English “tell me it”)
  • In negative commands they move in front of the verb (→ ②)

💡 TRIVIA　-s と -d が落ちるのは、発音しやすさのためです。levantemos＋nos では -mosnos と s と n がぶつかるので、-s が話し言葉の中で自然に脱落しました。levantad＋os の -d も同じように脱落し、その結果、過去分詞の levantados と同じ形になることも避けられています。唯一の例外は irse で、ios ではなく idos と -d を残すのが規範です。ただし口語では iros が広く使われ、RAE（スペイン王立アカデミー）も2017年にこの形を認めています。 TRIVIA　The -s and -d drop out for ease of pronunciation. In levantemos + nos the s and n collide (-mosnos), so speakers dropped the -s. The -d of levantad + os fell away the same way, which also avoids a clash with the past participle levantados. The one exception is irse: the standard form keeps the d — idos, not ios. In everyday speech iros is very common, and the RAE (Royal Spanish Academy) accepted it in 2017.

③ 接続法過去Imperfect subjunctive Pretérito imperfecto de subjuntivo

④ 接続法過去完了Pluperfect subjunctive Pretérito pluscuamperfecto de subjuntivo

This is the Spanish counterpart of English had + past participle in “If I had known…” and “I wish I had known”. English uses the ordinary past perfect there; Spanish uses its subjunctive version.

過去分詞は性・数による変化なし

The participle never changes for gender or number (just like English “had eaten”)

1. 時制の一致1. Sequence of tenses

主節が過去の時制（点過去・線過去・過去完了・過去未来・過去未来完了など）で、かつ接続法を要求するとき、従属節では接続法過去または接続法過去完了を使います。

When the main clause is in a past tense (preterite, imperfect, pluperfect, conditional, conditional perfect…) and it calls for the subjunctive, the subordinate clause uses the imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive. It is the same “backshift” you know from English reported speech (“I think he is” → “I thought he was”), applied to the subjunctive.

どちらを使うかWhich one?
  • 従属節の内容が主節の時点と同時ないし後 → 接続法過去
  • 従属節の内容が主節の時点よりも前 → 接続法過去完了

  • The subordinate action is earlier than the main verb → pluperfect subjunctive (English: “had + participle”)

⑥ 願望文Wishes (ojalá) Oraciones desiderativas

Ojalá (que) または Que ＋ 接続法で「〜だといいな」という願望を表します。どの接続法時制を使うかで実現可能性が変わるのがポイントです。

⑦ 条件文Conditional sentences Oraciones condicionales

「もし〜ならば…」を表す文です。現実的な仮定か、事実に反する仮定かで、条件節（Si〜）と帰結節の時制の組み合わせが決まります。

“If …, then …” sentences. Whether the condition is realistic or contrary to fact decides the pair of tenses in the if-clause (Si…) and the main clause. This is where English speakers have a head start: the three Spanish types are the English first, second and third conditionals.

1. 直説法の条件文：現実的なことを想定する1. Indicative conditionals: realistic situations (= first conditional)
条件節：Si ＋ 直説法現在　／　帰結節：直説法現在／直説法未来If-clause: Si + present indicative　/　Main clause: present or future indicative
Si tenemos tiempo, visitamos el jardín botánico.時間があれば、私たちは植物園を訪れる。If we have time, we visit the botanical garden.
Si llueve mañana, se suspenderá la excursión.明日雨が降れば、遠足は中止になるだろう。If it rains tomorrow, the trip will be called off.

※ Si の直後に直説法未来は置けない（× Si lloverá）。未来のことでも条件節は現在形。

※ Never put the future after si (× Si lloverá) — exactly like English “If it rains tomorrow”, not “If it will rain”.

2. 接続法過去の条件文：現在の事実に反すること／実現可能性がない・低いこと2. Imperfect-subjunctive conditionals: contrary to present fact / unlikely (= second conditional)
条件節：Si ＋ 接続法過去　／　帰結節：過去未来If-clause: Si + imperfect subjunctive (“If I had”)　/　Main clause: conditional (“I would”)
Si tuviéramos tiempo, visitaríamos el jardín botánico.もし時間があれば、植物園を訪れるのだが（実際は時間がない）。If we had time, we would visit the botanical garden (but we don’t).
Si los profesores no hicieran tantos exámenes, seríamos más felices.先生たちがそんなに試験をしなければ、私たちはもっと幸せなのに。If the teachers didn’t give so many exams, we would be happier.
3. 接続法過去完了の条件文：過去の事実に反することを仮定する3. Pluperfect-subjunctive conditionals: contrary to past fact (= third conditional)
条件節：Si ＋ 接続法過去完了　／　帰結節：過去未来完了If-clause: Si + pluperfect subjunctive (“If I had done”)　/　Main clause: conditional perfect (“I would have done”)

Rule of thumb: whenever English backshifts to “had” or “would” to show unreality, Spanish switches to a past subjunctive in the if-/wish-clause and to the -ría forms in the result clause.

2. Where English leads you astray
Trap 1 · want / ask / tell someone to doQuiero que vengas. ／ Le pedí que me ayudara.I want you to come. / I asked him to help me.English uses “object + to-infinitive”. Spanish cannot: × Quiero te venir, × Le pedí ayudarme. When the subject changes, use que + subjunctive, and after a past main verb, the imperfect subjunctive.
Trap 2 · negative commands change formHabla. → No hables. ／ Dímelo. → No me lo digas.Speak. → Don’t speak. / Tell me. → Don’t tell me.English just adds “don’t”. Spanish swaps the verb to the subjunctive and moves the pronouns in front of it. × No habla, × No dímelo.
Trap 3 · no “would” after si — but also no present subjunctiveSi me ayudaras, terminaríamos antes.If you would help me / If you helped me, we’d finish sooner.Polite English “if you would help me” tempts you into × Si me ayudarías. The conditional never goes after si; neither does the present subjunctive (× Si tengas). After si: present indicative or a past subjunctive only.
Trap 4 · “if + will” for willingnessSi espera aquí un momento, le atiendo enseguida.If you will wait here a moment, I’ll be right with you.English sometimes allows “if you will…” (= if you are willing). Spanish still uses the present indicative: × Si esperará.
Trap 5 · hope vs wishOjalá apruebes. ／ Ojalá aprobaras.I hope you pass. / I wish you would pass (but I doubt it).English chooses the verb; Spanish keeps ojalá and chooses the tense. Translating “I hope” as ojalá + imperfect subjunctive sounds pessimistic.
Trap 6 · the many meanings of “could”Podría ayudarte. ／ Si pudiera, te ayudaría. ／ No pude ayudarte.I could help you (possibility). / If I could, I would help you. / I couldn’t help you (past).One English word, three Spanish forms: conditional podría (possibility/politeness), imperfect subjunctive pudiera (after si, ojalá…), and preterite/imperfect pude/podía (real past ability).
Trap 7 · although vs even ifAunque llueve, salgo. ／ Aunque llueva, saldré.Although it is raining, I’m going out. / Even if it rains, I’ll go out.English changes the conjunction; Spanish keeps aunque and changes the mood. Using the indicative asserts that it is raining.
Trap 8 · one “you”, five command formsSiéntate. ／ Siéntese. ／ Sentaos. ／ Siéntense.Sit down. (tú / usted / vosotros / ustedes)English “Sit down, please” fits everyone. In Spanish, first decide formal or informal, singular or plural — getting it wrong is a register error, not just a spelling one.
Trap 9 · “let’s go”Vamos. ／ Vámonos. ／ No vayamos.Let’s go. / Let’s get going (let’s leave). / Let’s not go.For ir, the affirmative “let’s” is the indicative vamos (not the textbook vayamos); irse gives vámonos. Only the negative uses the subjunctive: no vayamos.
Self-check before you write a Spanish sentence from English
  • Is there an English “to-infinitive with a new subject” (want / ask / tell him to)? → que + subjunctive
  • Is the English using “had” or “would” to mark something unreal? → past subjunctive + -ría
  • Is it a negative command? → subjunctive, pronouns in front
  • Is there a “would” inside the if-clause? → delete it in Spanish

願望文Ojalá (que) / Que ＋ 接続法現在（可能性あり）／接続法過去（可能性低）／接続法過去完了（過去の反実）
条件文①現実的Si ＋ 直説法現在, 直説法現在／未来（Si llueve, se suspenderá）
条件文②現在の反実Si ＋ 接続法過去, 過去未来（Si tuviéramos tiempo, visitaríamos）
条件文③過去の反実Si ＋ 接続法過去完了, 過去未来完了（Si hubiéramos tenido…, habríamos visitado）
Si 節の禁止事項Si の直後に直説法未来・過去未来・接続法現在は置けない
譲歩文 aunque事実 → 直説法（Aunque hacía mal tiempo…）／仮定 → 接続法（Aunque estuviera…）
